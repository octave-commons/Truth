You can think of Phase 0 as “turning on” a coupled gravity–hydro–MHD–thermal system and then watching which regime dominates at each point in space. Below is a compact set of equations you can actually implement, plus the dimensionless diagnostics that tell you whether gravity, pressure, magnetic fields, or radiation are in charge locally, and how that carries through into planet interior convection, tectonics, and atmosphere–field coupling. [wwwmpa.mpa-garching.mpg](https://wwwmpa.mpa-garching.mpg.de/~henk/mhd12.pdf)

***

## Core field variables

Per cell (or voxel) you need at least:

- Mass density \( \rho \)
- Velocity \( \mathbf{v} \)
- Gas pressure \( p \)
- Internal energy per mass \( e \)
- Magnetic field \( \mathbf{B} \)
- Gravitational potential \( \Phi \)
- Radiation field / light energy density \( E_{\rm rad} \) (coarse)
- Composition, temperature \( T \), ionization fraction \( x_e \)

These live both in nebular gas and later in atmospheres and planetary interiors; the physics changes mainly via the parameters and dominant timescales, not the form of the equations.

## Stellar formation: gravity + MHD + thermodynamics

### Gravity and collapse

Self-gravity is set by Poisson’s equation:

\[
\nabla^2 \Phi = 4 \pi G \rho
\]

Gas feels gravity via the force \( -\rho \nabla \Phi \) in the momentum equation. [lweb.cfa.harvard](https://lweb.cfa.harvard.edu/~namurphy/Lectures/Ay253_2016_02_Ideal_MHD.pdf)

Collapse vs support is determined by Jeans criteria; the **Jeans length**:

\[
\lambda_J = \sqrt{\frac{\pi c_s^2}{G \rho}}, \quad c_s = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma p}{\rho}}
\]

Regions with size \( L > \lambda_J \) are gravitationally unstable and tend to collapse; smaller regions are pressure supported. [academic.oup](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/472/4/4155/4098504)

### Ideal MHD equations

For the magnetized nebula, the simplest full physics model is **ideal MHD** (plus cooling):

- **Continuity (mass conservation)**  
  \[
  \frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{v}) = 0
  \]

- **Momentum (Newton’s law for fluid)**  
  \[
  \rho\left(\frac{\partial \mathbf{v}}{\partial t} + \mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla \mathbf{v}\right)
   = -\nabla p - \rho \nabla \Phi + \frac{1}{4\pi}(\nabla \times \mathbf{B}) \times \mathbf{B}
  \]
  The last term is the Lorentz force, which can be decomposed into magnetic pressure and tension. [wwwmpa.mpa-garching.mpg](https://wwwmpa.mpa-garching.mpg.de/~henk/mhd12.pdf)

- **Induction (magnetic field evolution)**  
  Ideal Ohm’s law \( \mathbf{E} + \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B}/c = 0 \) combined with Faraday’s law gives:
  \[
  \frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t} = \nabla \times (\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B})
  \]
  plus the constraint \( \nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0 \). [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetohydrodynamics)

- **Energy (internal + kinetic + magnetic)**  
  In one convenient form:
  \[
  \frac{\partial e}{\partial t} + \mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla e
   = -\frac{p}{\rho} \nabla \cdot \mathbf{v} + \text{heating} - \text{cooling}
  \]
  where “heating” includes compressional heating, shocks, and nuclear burning once conditions are met; “cooling” is radiative and (later) neutrino cooling in very dense cores. [solar-mcs.wp.st-andrews.ac](http://solar-mcs.wp.st-andrews.ac.uk/files/2023/08/STFC_2023_MHD_Intro.pdf)

You can extend the induction equation with a resistive term \( \eta \nabla^2 \mathbf{B} \) for non-ideal MHD (ambipolar diffusion, Ohmic dissipation) once you want better flux removal in dense cores. [academic.oup](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/519/2/3076/6948347)

### Fusion ignition conditions

In your sink/Protostar object, you track central density and temperature via the same energy equation. Hydrogen burning starts once:

- Central \( T_c \sim 10^7 \,\mathrm{K} \)
- Sufficient density and composition for pp-chain or CNO cycles

At that point, nuclear power \( \epsilon_{\rm nuc}(\rho, T) \) enters the heating term and is allowed to balance radiative losses, halting contraction and placing the object on the main sequence. [astro1.physics.utoledo](http://astro1.physics.utoledo.edu/~megeath/ph6820/lecture20_ph6820.pdf)

## Planet formation in discs: Toomre Q and cooling

Once you have a protostellar disc, self-gravity vs pressure vs rotation is captured by the **Toomre Q parameter**: [users.monash](https://users.monash.edu/~dprice/teaching/ASP2062/L6-planetformation.pdf)

\[
Q(R) = \frac{c_s(R)\,\kappa(R)}{\pi G \Sigma(R)}
\]

- \( \Sigma(R) \) is disc surface density.  
- \( c_s(R) \) is sound speed (set by disc temperature).  
- \( \kappa(R) \) is epicyclic frequency (≈ orbital frequency \( \Omega \) in a Keplerian disc).

Key regime classifier:

- \( Q \gtrsim 1 \): disc stable against axisymmetric self-gravity at radius \( R \).  
- \( Q \lesssim 1 \): disc locally unstable; can form spiral arms and fragment into clumps/planets.  

Fragmentation also requires sufficiently fast cooling: Gammie’s criterion \( t_{\rm cool} \lesssim 3 \Omega^{-1} \) in thin discs, or the exact factor from your chosen cooling model. So per disc cell you can evaluate \( Q \) and \( t_{\rm cool} \) and decide whether the dominant regime is “smooth disc + core accretion” or “GI + clump formation”. [users.monash](https://users.monash.edu/~dprice/teaching/ASP2062/L6-planetformation.pdf)

Terrestrial planet growth, collisions, and migration can then be modeled with N-body gravity for planetesimals plus disc torques. You don’t need extra field equations—just gravitational interaction and drag/torque sourced by the disc’s hydrodynamics. [users.monash](https://users.monash.edu/~dprice/teaching/ASP2062/L6-planetformation.pdf)

## Planet interior: convection, mantle, core

By the end of Phase 0 you want a planet with:

- Molten or partially molten core
- Convecting mantle
- Surface volcanism and tectonics
- Global magnetic field and electric currents

### Mantle convection: Rayleigh number

Mantle convection is governed by the same physics as any heated fluid layer: buoyancy vs viscosity vs thermal diffusion. The key dimensionless number is the **Rayleigh number**: [egu](https://www.egu.eu/media/filer_public/e4/f8/e4f80bfa-55d3-4f28-b814-59f70beb678f/13_conrad.pdf)

\[
Ra = \frac{\rho g \alpha \Delta T d^3}{\mu \kappa}
\]

- \( \rho \) — density  
- \( g \) — gravity at mantle depth  
- \( \alpha \) — thermal expansivity  
- \( \Delta T \) — temperature contrast across the convecting layer  
- \( d \) — layer thickness (mantle depth)  
- \( \mu \) — dynamic viscosity  
- \( \kappa \) — thermal diffusivity  

Regimes:

- \( Ra < Ra_c \) (critical, ≈ O(10^3) for simple geometries): conduction dominates, no convection; planet is tectonically dead.  
- \( Ra > Ra_c \): convection occurs, with vigor increasing with \( Ra \); large \( Ra \) leads to turbulent, vigorous convection that can drive plate tectonics. [ijerd](http://www.ijerd.com/paper/vol12-issue3/Version-2/B12321021.pdf)

The underlying **energy equation** in the mantle is:

\[
\rho c_p \left( \frac{\partial T}{\partial t} + \mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla T \right)
 = k \nabla^2 T + H_{\rm rad} - H_{\rm loss}
\]

- \( c_p \) — heat capacity; \( k \) — thermal conductivity.  
- \( H_{\rm rad} \) — volumetric heating (radioactive decay, latent heat).  
- \( H_{\rm loss} \) — heat loss to surface and core.

Solving this in combination with Stokes flow (\( \nabla \cdot \sigma + \rho \mathbf{g} = 0 \)) gives mantle convection patterns that drive volcanism and tectonics. [earth.yale](https://earth.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2024-08/Bercovici%20doc%2045.pdf)

### Core dynamo: induction + rotation

The planetary magnetic field is generated by a convecting, electrically conducting fluid core. At core scales you’re again in MHD; the same induction equation applies: [users.camk.edu](https://users.camk.edu.pl/knalew/cmf22/cmf_lecture_mhd.pdf)

\[
\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t} = \nabla \times (\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B}) + \eta \nabla^2 \mathbf{B}
\]

with:

- \( \mathbf{v} \) — core flow velocity, influenced by rotation and buoyancy.  
- \( \eta = 1/(\mu_0 \sigma) \) — magnetic diffusivity, \(\sigma\) electrical conductivity.

Regimes:

- If convective power (from core cooling, compositional convection) and rotation are large enough, the dynamo sustains a global dipole; that’s your “strong magnetic umbrella” case.  
- If convection is weak or core is solidified, \( \mathbf{B} \) decays, leaving only crustal/remanent fields; the planet behaves like Mars/Venus in its interaction with the stellar wind. [oxfordre](https://oxfordre.com/planetaryscience/display/10.1093/acrefore/9780190647926.001.0001/acrefore-9780190647926-e-184?d=%2F10.1093%2Facrefore%2F9780190647926.001.0001%2Facrefore-9780190647926-e-184&p=emailAQXqTnIJpiLEI)

You don’t need to simulate full dynamo turbulence—just ensure that core convection and rotation feed a field consistent with these regimes.

## Atmosphere: gas + gravity + EM + light

Now combine mantle/core with atmosphere and stellar fields.

### Hydrostatic structure and circulation

Vertical structure is set by hydrostatic equilibrium:

\[
\frac{dp}{dz} = -\rho g
\]

with an equation of state \( p = \rho k_B T / \mu \) and radiative-convective energy balance. Large-scale circulation uses the Navier–Stokes equations on a rotating sphere:

\[
\frac{\partial \mathbf{v}}{\partial t} + \mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}
 = -\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla p - 2 \mathbf{\Omega} \times \mathbf{v} + \mathbf{g} + \text{viscosity} + \text{EM forces}
\]

where \( \mathbf{\Omega} \) is planetary rotation, and “EM forces” include Lorentz terms on ionized components. [cglearn](https://cglearn.eu/pub/advanced-computer-graphics/volumetric-rendering)

### Solar wind and plasma interaction

The star’s wind interacts with planetary fields and atmospheres via MHD and kinetic processes. At a coarse level: [ntrs.nasa](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19960047094)

- Momentum equation gets an extra term where charged atmospheric particles feel \( \mathbf{J} \times \mathbf{B} \) and electric fields.  
- Energy equation gets heating terms (Joule heating, particle precipitation) in the upper atmosphere.  

Weakly magnetized planets show strong ion escape and plasma transport:

- Solar wind dynamic pressure compresses the dayside ionosphere.  
- Magnetic field draping and reconnection near the terminator lead to plasma escape on the nightside.  
- Empirical or reduced models give escape rates as functions of EUV flux, wind pressure, and EM geometry. [oxfordre](https://oxfordre.com/planetaryscience/display/10.1093/acrefore/9780190647926.001.0001/acrefore-9780190647926-e-184?d=%2F10.1093%2Facrefore%2F9780190647926.001.0001%2Facrefore-9780190647926-e-184&p=emailAQXqTnIJpiLEI)

In Truth, you can evaluate:

- Whether the planet is in a **strongly magnetized regime** (core dynamo active, large dipole).  
- Whether the atmosphere is **highly ionized and coupled** or mostly neutral.  

and then let EM terms dominate upper-atmosphere behavior only where ionization and field strength are high.

## Local dominance: regime classifier

All of these equations can run everywhere, but you don’t want to simulate full MHD + detailed mantle convection where it doesn’t matter. Instead, compute simple dimensionless numbers per cell and choose dominant physics:

- **Plasma beta** (gas vs magnetic pressure):  
  \[
  \beta = \frac{P_{\rm gas}}{P_B}
   = \frac{p}{B^2 / 8\pi}
  \]
  - \( \beta \gg 1 \): gas pressure dominates; gravity + hydro.  
  - \( \beta \ll 1 \): magnetic field dominates; EM shaping flows. [users.camk.edu](https://users.camk.edu.pl/knalew/cmf22/cmf_lecture_mhd.pdf)

- **Mach number** (supersonic vs subsonic):  
  \[
  \mathcal{M} = \frac{|\mathbf{v}|}{c_s}
  \]
  - \( \mathcal{M} \gg 1 \): shocks, compressible turbulence.  
  - \( \mathcal{M} \ll 1 \): nearly incompressible flow.

- **Alfvén Mach number** (flow vs Alfvén speed):  
  \[
  M_A = \frac{|\mathbf{v}|}{v_A}, \quad v_A = \frac{B}{\sqrt{4\pi \rho}}
  \]
  - \( M_A \ll 1 \): magnetic tension and pressure strongly constrain motion.  
  - \( M_A \gg 1 \): magnetic field is passively advected. [wwwmpa.mpa-garching.mpg](https://wwwmpa.mpa-garching.mpg.de/~henk/mhd12.pdf)

- **Gravitational instability** (Jeans or Toomre Q):  
  - Jeans \( L/\lambda_J \) and disc \( Q \).  

- **Cooling vs dynamical time**:  
  \[
  \frac{t_{\rm cool}}{t_{\rm dyn}} \quad \text{with} \quad t_{\rm dyn} \sim \Omega^{-1} \text{ or free-fall time}
  \]
  Fragmentation needs fast cooling. [users.monash](https://users.monash.edu/~dprice/teaching/ASP2062/L6-planetformation.pdf)

- **Rayleigh number** (mantle convection):  
  \[
  Ra = \frac{\rho g \alpha \Delta T d^3}{\mu \kappa}
  \]
  - \( Ra < Ra_c \): conduction; tectonically quiet.  
  - \( Ra \gg Ra_c \): vigorous convection; tectonics, volcanism. [perso.univ-lyon1](https://perso.univ-lyon1.fr/yanick.ricard/Article/113.pdf)

At each location and scale (nebula cell, disc cell, mantle cell, atmosphere voxel), compute these numbers and have your engine choose:

- “Gravity–hydro” regime (Jeans unstable, \( \beta \gg 1 \), \( M_A \gg 1 \)).  
- “MHD-dominated” regime (\( \beta \ll 1 \), \( M_A \lesssim 1 \)).  
- “Radiation-dominated” regime (high \( E_{\rm rad} \), strong cooling).  
- “Convection/tectonics” regime (mantle \( Ra \gg Ra_c \)).  

The equations are always present; the regime logic tells you which terms to prioritize for simulation detail and narrative.
