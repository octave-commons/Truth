There's one model, not two — but it's a degenerate fluid model

Gas is SPH particles (hydro.clj). And here's the thing that dissolves your headache: "particles" vs "field" is a false dichotomy. SPH (Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics) is specifically a way to represent a continuous fluid field using particles — each particle is a moving sample of the field, not a little rock. Real astrophysical star-formation codes (Gadget, etc.) do exactly this. So the summary saying "equal-mass gas particles" and "pressure, density, composition" is not a contradiction; that's just what SPH is.

But your instinct that something is off is correct, and sharper than the summary. The implementation is an incomplete SPH, and the field is degenerate:

- In real SPH, a particle's density is computed from its neighbors (ρᵢ = Σ mⱼ W) — that's the whole point; density rises where particles crowd. This code never does that. Density is mass / sphere-volume with a fixed radius (prad), set once at seeding (stellar.clj body-density).
- For :nebula gas, mass is fixed, rre is held constant (thermal-system
returns temp unchanged for nebula).constant and identical for every gas particle.
- That means the "pressure field" is uniform. The hydro force becomes a uniform short-range anti-overlap repulsion, carrying no real structure.

So your observation — "they all render the same size, but if they had different pressures…" — is exactly right: they don't have different pressures. There's no pressure field to see yet.
